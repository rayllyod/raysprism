# Linear periodization

*The classic model: start a training block with higher volume and lower intensity, and gradually shift toward lower volume and higher intensity as the block progresses toward a peak. Simple to plan, easy to track, and it works well for beginners who are still adapting to almost any structured load.*

# Undulating periodization

*Instead of one long ramp, volume and intensity vary session to session or week to week — a heavy day, a volume day, a light day, repeating in a shorter cycle. More responsive to how recovery is actually going, at the cost of being harder to plan on a spreadsheet in advance.*

# Deload weeks

*A planned week of reduced volume or intensity, placed before fatigue forces an unplanned one. The point isn't to detrain — a few days under lighter load doesn't undo weeks of adaptation — it's to let accumulated fatigue clear before it turns into a missed lift or an overuse injury.*
