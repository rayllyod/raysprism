# The principle

*Muscle adapts to the specific demand placed on it, and stops adapting once that demand stops increasing. Same weight, same reps, same sets, week after week — the stimulus goes flat and so does the result. Overload has to be progressive: a little more weight, a little more volume, or a little more effort each cycle, or the plateau shows up right on schedule.*

# How to apply it

*The lever doesn't have to be load on the bar. Reps at the same weight, sets at the same reps, shorter rest, better range of motion, or slower eccentrics all count as overload. Picking one lever at a time makes it obvious which one actually moved the number, instead of changing five variables and guessing at what worked.*

# Where it breaks down

*Overload without recovery just becomes fatigue. Adding load every single session eventually outpaces what sleep, food, and joints can absorb, and the plateau it was supposed to prevent shows up anyway — as an injury instead of a stall.*
